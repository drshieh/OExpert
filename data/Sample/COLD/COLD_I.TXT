Common
The common cold and influenza, or flu, are both
viral diseases. A cold causes fatgue, a scratchy
or sore throat, hoarseness, coughing, sneezing,
a runny or stuffy nose, watering eyes, and
headaches, but it usually does not cause a fever
over 100 &suo&sdF. Cold symptoms usually last
from 5 to 7 days.

Flu
Flu symptoms are initially the same as those of
a cold, but they become more severe, causing
fever in adults, and even higher fever in
children.  Flu sufferers also experience muscle
and joint pain, weakness, and loss of appetite.
Flu symptoms may disappear in 1 to 2 weeks, but
full recovery can take from 2 to 3 weeks. There
are no cures for cold or flu infections, but you
can relieve some of the symptoms.

Immunization
Those in the high-risk groups - for example, the
elderly and those with chronic cardiac or
respiratory conditions - should be immunized
against the flu, since it is far more dangerous
for these people.

Sever Symptoms
Be sure you don't mistake cold and flu symptoms
for those of another disease or condition.
Contact your physician if you perceive any of
these symptoms:
  1. An earache or blurry vision.
  2. Wheezing or hard and rapid breathing with
     flared nostrils.
  3. A heavy, prolonged cough, one with cloudy
     mucus, or one that lasts more than a week.
  4. A fever that does not come down with
     treatment in 2 or 3 days.
  5. A sore throat with swollen glands and
     fever over 100.5 &suo&sdF that doesn't improve
     within 2 or 3 days.
  6. A fever of 102 &suo&sdF for 24 hours or longer.
  7. A stiff neck or a rash.
  8. Severe abdominal pan or moderate abdominal
     pain that lasts longer than 4 to 6 hours.
  9. Chest pain or shortness of breath for more
     than 10 minutes.
 10. Disorientation or loss of coordination or
     consciousness.
 11. Excessive sleepiness.

Children
If the patient is under 2.5 years old and is
running a fever over 102 &suo&sdF at any time, contact
the doctor immediately.
